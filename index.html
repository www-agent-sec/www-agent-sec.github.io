About

WASST 2027 brings together the web, security, and machine learning communities to advance the safety, security, and trustworthiness of generative AI agents on the web.

Generative AI agents are moving from research prototypes to systems that browse websites, operate graphical interfaces, call external tools, and complete transactions with little to no human intervention. As agents become more autonomous, they introduce new risks — and new questions for the web community.

The workshop creates a shared forum for understanding these risks, developing practical defences, and anticipating emerging threats. We welcome work on deployed systems as well as forward-looking research, with substantial space for discussion across disciplines and career stages.

Topics

The workshop is organised around three complementary dimensions — safety, security, and trust — alongside the ecosystems, defences, and evaluation practices that connect them. Topics include, but are not limited to:

Safety

Failures that arise without an adversary, including misunderstood instructions, unintended or irreversible actions, and compounding errors across long action sequences.

  • Unintended and irreversible actions
  • Error propagation in long-horizon and multi-agent tasks
  • Failures of safety alignment in tool use and GUI execution
  • Privacy leakage through agent actions

Security

Deliberate attacks that exploit web content, tools, and interfaces to hijack, manipulate, or exhaust agents.

  • Attacks across the perception, reasoning, execution, and memory layers of web agents
  • Prompt injection, goal hijacking, and confused-deputy exploits
  • Backdoor and poisoning attacks
  • Multimodal and interface-based jailbreaks
  • Memory poisoning and persistent attacks
  • Resource-exhaustion and denial-of-efficiency attacks
  • Automated and adaptive red-teaming

Trust

Agents deployed for harmful ends, and the web’s ability to distinguish them from legitimate agents.

  • Agents deployed for fraud, manipulation, abusive automation, or attacks on web services
  • Detection and measurement of agent activity at web scale
  • Agent identity, authentication, authorization, and attestation
  • Content provenance and non-repudiation
  • Effects of agentic traffic on publishers, platforms, and users

Agent ecosystems and emerging threats

  • Security of tool and agent supply chains
  • Malicious or compromised tools, plugins, and MCP servers
  • Tool poisoning, privilege escalation, and cross-agent attacks
  • Fully autonomous agents conducting fraud, scams, and social engineering at scale
  • Coordinated bot networks operating across platforms
  • Risks arising from multi-agent systems, agentic interaction, and tool protocols such as MCP
  • Attacks on retrieval-augmented and memory-persistent agents
  • Threats related to agentic commerce and autonomous transactions

Defences, evaluation, and governance

  • Benchmarks, datasets, and metrics for agent safety, security, and trust
  • Runtime monitoring, containment, and policy enforcement
  • Least-privilege tool access and permission control
  • Auditing, traceability, and incident response
  • Empirical studies of commercial agents in real web environments
  • Scalable evaluation against adaptive adversaries
  • Open problems and forward-looking position papers

Submission Requirements

We encourage contributions that address current challenges in web agents, anticipate emerging threats, or set out new research directions. Research on deployed systems, work in progress, and forward-looking studies and position papers are welcome.

The Web Conference 2027 — Workshops

Format of Workshop

A proposed full-day, in-person workshop combining contributed talks, a poster session, a panel, and a closing discussion. The programme is organised around security, safety, and trust for agents on the web, with substantial time for questions and discussion.

Attendance

The workshop welcomes researchers and practitioners from web research, security, machine learning, information retrieval, and related disciplines, across academia and industry. Early-career researchers and work in progress are particularly encouraged.

We expect 50–60 on-site participants. The proposed programme includes a poster presentation for every accepted paper, including those selected for oral presentation. Please refer to the official attending information for participation and registration updates.
