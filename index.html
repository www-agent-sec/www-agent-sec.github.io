About

A growing share of web activity is now performed by AI agents acting on people’s behalf. This is reshaping the risk landscape of the web. Agents can make mistakes with real-world consequences, be manipulated through untrusted web content, or be deliberately used to defraud users and abuse web services. Addressing these risks requires expertise across multiple research disciplines.

WASST 2027 brings together researchers and practitioners from academia and industry across these disciplines, focusing on three themes. Safety covers agent failures that occur without an adversary. Security covers attacks that hijack or manipulate agents through web content, tools and interfaces. Trust covers malicious agents and how to distinguish them from legitimate ones. We welcome contributions on methods, evaluation, resources, governance and societal implications. We particularly encourage contributions from early-career researchers and industry.

Topics

The workshop is organised around three complementary dimensions — safety, security, and trust — alongside the ecosystems, defences, and evaluation practices that connect them. Topics include, but are not limited to:

Safety

Failures that arise without an adversary, including misunderstood instructions, unintended or irreversible actions, and compounding errors across long action sequences.

  • Unintended and irreversible actions
  • Error propagation in long-horizon and multi-agent tasks
  • Failures of safety alignment in tool use and GUI execution
  • Privacy leakage through agent actions

Security

Deliberate attacks that exploit web content, tools, and interfaces to hijack, manipulate, or exhaust agents.

  • Attacks across the perception, reasoning, execution, and memory layers of web agents
  • Prompt injection, goal hijacking, and confused-deputy exploits
  • Backdoor and poisoning attacks
  • Multimodal and interface-based jailbreaks
  • Memory poisoning and persistent attacks
  • Resource-exhaustion and denial-of-efficiency attacks
  • Automated and adaptive red-teaming

Trust

Agents deployed for harmful ends, and the web’s ability to distinguish them from legitimate agents.

  • Agents deployed for fraud, manipulation, abusive automation, or attacks on web services
  • Detection and measurement of agent activity at web scale
  • Agent identity, authentication, authorization, and attestation
  • Content provenance and non-repudiation
  • Effects of agentic traffic on publishers, platforms, and users

Agent ecosystems and emerging threats

  • Security of tool and agent supply chains
  • Malicious or compromised tools, plugins, and MCP servers
  • Tool poisoning, privilege escalation, and cross-agent attacks
  • Fully autonomous agents conducting fraud, scams, and social engineering at scale
  • Coordinated bot networks operating across platforms
  • Risks arising from multi-agent systems, agentic interaction, and tool protocols such as MCP
  • Attacks on retrieval-augmented and memory-persistent agents
  • Threats related to agentic commerce and autonomous transactions

Defences, evaluation, and governance

  • Benchmarks, datasets, and metrics for agent safety, security, and trust
  • Runtime monitoring, containment, and policy enforcement
  • Least-privilege tool access and permission control
  • Auditing, traceability, and incident response
  • Empirical studies of commercial agents in real web environments
  • Scalable evaluation against adaptive adversaries
  • Open problems and forward-looking position papers

Submission Requirements

Submit your paper through WASST 2027 on OpenReview by 4 January 2027, 00:00 UTC. Both submission tracks use the same deadline.

Choose a track and check the page limit

  • Archival track (for publication). A long paper may have up to 8 pages, and a short paper up to 4 pages. For both lengths, references and appendices are excluded from the limit. Submit original work that has not been published and is not under review elsewhere.
  • Non-archival track (for presentation). Papers may have up to 8 pages. Work already published or under review at another journal, conference, or workshop is eligible. An accepted paper is presented at WASST and does not appear in the conference proceedings.

Prepare an anonymous PDF

Use the ACM sigconf format and upload your manuscript as a PDF. The technical chairs lead a double-blind peer review by the workshop program committee. The following requirements apply to both tracks. Get the ACM proceedings template.

  • Remove all author names and affiliations from the manuscript.
  • Cite your own work without identifying yourself as its author.
  • Omit links to repositories or websites that reveal an author’s identity.

Submissions that are not properly anonymised will be desk rejected.

Complete the OpenReview form

Every author must have an OpenReview profile before submission. Enter the authors in the form and keep the manuscript anonymous. Provide the paper title, comma-separated keywords, an abstract of up to 5,000 characters, and the PDF. The optional one-sentence summary (TLDR) may be up to 250 characters.

Authorship and generative AI

All submissions must follow the ACM Policy on Authorship. Generative AI tools cannot be listed as authors. If you used a generative AI tool to produce content for the paper, disclose that use in the manuscript, for example in the acknowledgements.

Reciprocal reviewing

At least one author of each submission may be required to review papers for WASST. Authors who are invited but fail to review responsibly may have their own submissions desk rejected.

Publication Policy

Archival papers and the camera-ready version

Accepted archival papers are included in the Companion Proceedings of The Web Conference 2027, subject to the camera-ready deadline. Acceptance notifications are scheduled for 1 February 2027. If your archival paper is accepted, submit the final version by 16 February 2027 for inclusion in the proceedings.

Prior publication and simultaneous submissions

The archival track prohibits dual submission. If your paper has already been published or is under review elsewhere, you may submit it to the non-archival track for presentation only. The conference states its policy as follows.

Papers accepted by a workshop can be included in the Companion Proceedings of the Web Conference 2027, subject to meeting the camera-ready timeline. Workshop papers that have been previously published or are under review for another journal, conference or workshop should not be considered for publication but can be presented in a non-archival workshop.

Attendance

The workshop welcomes researchers and practitioners from web research, security, machine learning, information retrieval, and related disciplines, across academia and industry. Early-career researchers and work in progress are particularly encouraged.

We expect 50–60 on-site participants. The proposed programme includes a poster presentation for every accepted paper, including those selected for oral presentation. For questions about attending or presenting, contact the workshop organisers.
